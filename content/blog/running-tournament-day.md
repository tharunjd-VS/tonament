The draw is the easy part. The day itself is where tournaments run late: one pair stuck in traffic holds up a court, a walk-in arrives with cash, someone twists an ankle at 15-12. A good organiser doesn't avoid these — they have a rule for each one, decided in advance.

## Run an order of play, not a timetable

A fixed timetable ("Court 2, 10:40") breaks the first time a match runs long. An **order of play** works better: a single queue of every match across every event, in the order they should be played. When a court comes free, the next *ready* match goes on.

A match is ready when both sides are here and neither is playing on another court. That last part is what catches people out: a player in both Men's Doubles and Mixed Doubles can't be on Court 1 and Court 3 at once, so their next match waits and the one after it goes on instead.

Give players an **estimated start time** — "about 11:20" — rather than a promise, and ask them to stay close when they're within two matches.

On Tonament the Courts tab is that queue: what's on each court, who's up next with estimated times, and a button to call the next match to a free court (or let it call automatically when a match ends). The venue TV shows the same thing, so the crowd at the desk thins out.

## When a player isn't here yet

**Rule:** their match waits; the court takes the next ready match.

Don't hold a court empty for ten minutes — that delay multiplies across the day. Mark them *not here*, let the next match on, and bring theirs back as soon as they arrive. Set a limit (say 15 minutes from their call) after which it becomes a walkover, and announce it at the start of the day.

On Tonament: tap **Not here** on the called match. Their matches wait in the order of play, and **Here ✓** brings them back.

## When a player never comes

**Rule:** a walkover to the opponent, after your published limit.

In a group, a walkover counts as a win for the opponent. In a knockout, the opponent goes through. Record it as a walkover rather than inventing a score — it keeps the standings honest.

On Tonament: **Walkover to …** on the waiting match.

## When a player entered but missed the draw

You made the draw at 8:30 with the players who were checked in. At 9:15 a pair who *had* entered walks in.

**Rule:** add them to a group that hasn't finished, with catch-up matches against everyone in it — or a bye into the knockout if it's knockout only. Choose the group with the fewest players, or in a level-split event, the group for their level.

On Tonament: tap **Arrived** on their entry and choose the group; their catch-up matches join the order of play.

## Spot entries

A walk-in with the entry fee in hand. Decide in advance whether you take spot entries and until when (usually until the first round of groups is half done). They join a group exactly like a late arrival. Take the fee by UPI or cash at the desk and mark it paid straight away.

## Injuries and retirements

**Rule:** the player retires; the opponent wins, and the score so far is kept.

A retirement is different from a walkover: the match started, and the points played count for any tie-break on game or point difference. On Tonament, **Retire** ends the match with the score at that moment.

## A substitute partner

A doubles player can't make it and their partner brings a friend. Decide whether you allow substitutes (most club events do, before the pair's first match, and at the same level). If you do, simply rename the pair — the results stay with the entry.

## Changing the order

Sometimes you need a particular match next: a player has to leave early, a final needs the show court. Pin that match to the front of the queue and unpin it afterwards. Keep it rare — players notice when the order of play bends for some people.

## When everything runs late

- **Shorten the groups:** a single game to 21 instead of best of 3.
- **Use more courts for the knockout** once the groups finish.
- **Play the plate or consolation event another day.**

Tell players early. A message at noon saying "we're running 30 minutes late" is far better than an angry desk at 4 pm.

## Write your rules down

Before the day, publish your rules on the tournament page: reporting time, the walkover limit, substitutes, spot entries, shuttles. Then every decision on the day is applying a rule, not making one up — and nobody argues with a rule they were told about.
