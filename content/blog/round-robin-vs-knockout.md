The format you pick decides three things: how many matches each player gets, how long the day runs, and how fair the result feels. Here is how the three common formats compare — with the numbers worked out.

## Knockout (single elimination)

Lose once and you're out. Seeds are placed so the best players meet as late as possible, and the bracket is filled to the next power of two (8, 16, 32…) with **byes** for the top seeds.

- **Matches:** one fewer than the number of entries. 24 players play 23 matches.
- **Rounds:** 5 for a 32-draw, 4 for a 16-draw.
- **Good for:** big opens, short days, a clear champion.
- **The catch:** half the field plays once and goes home — after paying the same entry fee.

Try the [knockout bracket maker](/tools/knockout-bracket) to see where the seeds and byes land.

## Round robin (everyone plays everyone)

Every entry plays every other entry once; the table decides the winner.

- **Matches:** n × (n − 1) ÷ 2. Six players play 15 matches; ten play 45.
- **Rounds:** n − 1 for an even number of players, n for an odd number (one bye per round).
- **Good for:** small events, leagues over several weeks, ladders.
- **The catch:** it grows fast. Twelve players would need 66 matches.

The [round robin generator](/tools/round-robin) lists every round, with byes.

## Groups, then a knockout (League + Knockout)

The best of both, and the format most club tournaments use: small round-robin groups, then the top one or two from each group go into a seeded knockout.

- **Matches for 16 entries in four groups of four:** 24 group matches + 7 knockout matches (top two through) = **31**.
- **Everyone plays at least three matches.**
- **Groups are seeded in a snake** (1–4 into groups A–D, 5–8 back from D to A), so the top seeds are in different groups.
- **In the knockout**, two players from the same group are kept apart in the first round.

## Side by side: 16 entries

| Format | Matches | Each player plays | About how long on 4 courts (30 min a match) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Knockout | 15 | 1 – 4 | 2½ h |
| Groups of 4 + knockout (top 2) | 31 | 3 – 6 | 4½ h |
| Round robin, one group | 120 | 15 | 15 h |

## Tie-breaks in a group

Two players finish level on wins. Decide the order before the event — and publish it. A common order:

1. Matches won (or league points: win 2, draw 1).
2. Head-to-head, when two players are tied.
3. Game difference, then point difference.

On paper this is where the evening goes. On Tonament the group tables apply these automatically as each score comes in.

## Draws in a league

Some leagues play exactly **two games** per match. 1-1 is a draw, and the table ranks on points: win 2, draw 1, loss 0. It keeps league nights to time, and you can still play the knockout best of three.

## Which should you choose?

- **A club night with 8–12 players:** one round robin group, single games to 21.
- **A club open with 16–32 entries per event:** groups of four, top two into a knockout.
- **A big open with 64+ entries:** a straight knockout, or groups of three with only the winner going through.
- **A mixed-ability Open:** groups by level — see [splitting an Open event by level](/blog/split-open-event-by-level).
- **A weekly league:** a round robin across weeks, with 2-game matches and draws.
