A good club badminton tournament feels effortless to the players. Behind it there is a plan made a few weeks earlier and a handful of decisions on the day. This guide walks through both — whether you run it on paper, on Tonament, or a bit of each.

## Four weeks before: decide the shape

**Pick your events.** The usual set for a club open is Men's Singles, Men's Doubles, Women's Doubles and Mixed Doubles. Add age groups (U15, 35+, 45+) if you have the players, and think about level: an *Open* event with beginners and state-level players in the same draw is no fun for anyone. Either run separate level events, or [split the Open by level](/blog/split-open-event-by-level).

**Count your court time.** This is the number that decides everything else.

| Your day | Example |
|---|---|
| Courts | 4 |
| Hours | 9 am – 7 pm = 10 hours |
| Minutes per match (best of 3 to 21) | about 30 |
| Matches you can fit | 4 × 10 × 60 ÷ 30 = **80** |

A group of four plays 6 matches. Sixteen pairs in four groups is 24 group matches plus 7 in the knockout — 31 matches, about four and a half hours on four courts. Our [round robin generator](/tools/round-robin) does this arithmetic for you.

If the numbers don't fit, play groups to a single game of 21, or 2 games with draws allowed, and keep best of 3 for the knockout.

**Choose a format.** Groups of four feeding a knockout is the club favourite: everyone gets three matches, and the top one or two go through. For a big open, a straight knockout. For a ladder or social night, a league. There's a full comparison in [round robin vs knockout](/blog/round-robin-vs-knockout).

## Three weeks before: open entries

Put out one message with everything a player needs: date, venue, events, fee, what's included (shuttles, water, lunch, a T-shirt), and how to enter. Then collect entries in one place.

- **Doubles partners:** ask for both names. Players without a partner should be able to say so, and you pair them up before the draw.
- **Fees:** collect by UPI to your number and keep a clear paid / unpaid list. [Collecting entry fees by UPI](/blog/collecting-entry-fees-upi) covers how to do it without chasing.
- **Limits:** set a maximum per event, with a waitlist for the rest.

On Tonament this is one link: players enter from their phones, pay the fee to your UPI, and you confirm with a tap. Full events fill a waitlist automatically.

## One week before: seed and draw

**Seed** by listing entries best first. Seeds should be spread across groups (a snake: 1, 2, 3, 4 into groups A–D, then 5–8 back from D to A) and the top seeds should get any byes in the knockout.

**Make the draw** once entries close — but expect to redo it. Somebody always drops out. On paper that means rewriting the chart; on Tonament you redo the draw in a tap, any time before the first point is played.

**Publish** the fixtures with estimated times, so players know when to arrive. Ask everyone to report 30 minutes before their first match.

## The day before: courts, umpires and shuttles

- **Courts:** number them, and decide which events start where.
- **Umpires:** for a club event, the losing pair of the previous match often umpires the next one. Each umpire needs a scoresheet — or a phone with the match open.
- **Shuttles:** count roughly one tube per six to eight matches for feather shuttles, and say which ones you're using.
- **The desk:** one person, one list, one place for "when am I playing?"

## On the day

**Check in** players as they arrive, and draw only those who are actually there if you're making the draw that morning.

**Run an order of play**, not a fixed timetable. Matches go on the next free court in order, skipping anyone who's still playing elsewhere. Players in two events will be on two courts at once if you're not careful — the order of play has to know about every event at once.

**Expect the unexpected:**

- someone isn't here yet — let the next ready match go on, and theirs waits;
- someone never comes — a walkover;
- an injury mid-match — a retirement, with the score kept;
- a substitute partner — rename the pair;
- a walk-in with cash — a spot entry into a group, with catch-up matches.

[Running the day](/blog/running-tournament-day) goes through each of these.

**Score it.** Umpires call the score, the server and the court; the desk records the result and updates the group table. Two things go wrong on paper: scores carried to the desk get mixed up, and the group tables (with head-to-head tie-breaks) take ages. On Tonament, umpires tap the side that won each rally and everything updates itself — players and parents follow the score from the stands.

## After the final

Announce the winners, take the photos, and publish the results the same evening. Players love seeing their name — and on Tonament the results go on each player's profile and into the [rankings](/rankings), which is the best advertisement for your next tournament.

## A short checklist

1. Events, levels and a court-time budget.
2. One entry link, fees by UPI, a paid list.
3. Seeding, the draw, and estimated times.
4. Courts, umpires, shuttles, a desk.
5. An order of play — and a plan for late players.
6. Results published the same day.
