Entry fees are the least fun part of running a tournament. The money arrives in four ways — UPI to your number, UPI to your friend's number, cash at the desk, "I'll pay on the day" — and you spend the week before matching screenshots to names. Here's a system that keeps it simple, with or without an app.

## Decide the rules first

Write these into your announcement:

- **The fee per event** — per player for singles, per pair for doubles, per team (or per player) for team events.
- **How to pay** — one UPI ID, with the name players will see in their UPI app.
- **What to write in the note** — the player's name and the event, e.g. "Asha MD".
- **When it's due** — "within 48 hours of entering, or your place goes to the waitlist".
- **Cash** — whether you accept it, and until when.
- **Refunds** — usually none after the draw; a full refund if you cancel the event.

## One UPI ID, one list

Use **one UPI ID** for the whole tournament, ideally a separate one from your personal account so the payments are easy to find. Keep **one list** of entries with a paid column. Every payment goes against a name on that list — nothing else counts as paid.

The **UPI reference** (the 12-digit number in every UPI payment's details) is what makes matching easy. Ask players to send it, and you can search your bank app for it instead of scrolling through screenshots.

## Hold places, briefly

If entries are limited, a place should be **held** while the player pays — but not for ever. 24 or 48 hours is usual; after that the place goes to the next player on the waitlist. It stops the "I'll pay later" entries from blocking real ones.

## Pairs and teams

**Doubles:** decide whether one partner pays for the pair or each pays their half — and record which half is paid.

**Teams:** the captain usually collects from the players and pays the whole fee, but for larger teams it's often easier to mark each player as paid. A team is fully paid when every player is settled (or waived).

## On the day

Expect a few cash payments and a few walk-ins. Keep the desk list open, mark cash payments straight away, and don't let anyone play their first match until their fee is marked — it's much harder to collect after they've lost.

If you change the fee on the day (a late-entry fee, say), apply it only to people who haven't paid yet.

## How it works on Tonament

Tonament puts all of this on the Players tab — and **never handles the money**: players pay you directly.

1. **Set the fee** for each event in Setup › Dates, venue & fees, with your UPI ID, the payee name, and (optionally) your UPI QR code and a note like "cash is also fine at the desk".
2. **Players enter**, and their UPI app opens with your UPI ID and the amount filled in. On a computer they scan your QR code. They tap **I've paid** and can add the 12-digit UPI reference.
3. **You confirm** each payment from the "Payments to check" list: **Received**, **Not received**, **Paid in cash** or **Waive**. Their place is confirmed.
4. **Places are held** for 24 hours, 48 hours or until entries close while unpaid — your choice.
5. **Pairs and teams** can be marked partner by partner or player by player; team events can charge per team or per player.
6. **On the day**, mark walk-ins paid in cash, change the fee if you need to (unpaid entries owe the new amount; payments already made stay as they are), and undo a payment marked by mistake.

The total collected is always on the entries card, so the reconciliation at the end of the day is one number.
