If you've run a club tournament in India, you know the sheet. A long notebook page or a roll of chart paper taped to the wall. Pairs grouped by level in the margin, ticks for who has paid, a name crossed out with an arrow to a substitute, "not here??" scribbled next to a time, and the semi-final pairings circled at the bottom.

:::paper Open — Men's Doubles ₹600
OPEN — Men's Doubles ₹600
BEG: Arun/Bala ✓ · Charan/Dinesh ✓
INT: Mohan/Naveen ✓ · ~~Om/Prakash~~ ==→ Ravi==
==Arun/Bala — not here?? 10:40==
==Om v Sanjay — W/O ?==
₹ paid: 9 of 13 … chase Kavin
((SF: BEG v BEG+ · INT v ADV))
:::

That sheet is genuinely good at one thing: **flexibility**. You can split an Open event by level because you know the players. You can push a match back when someone is stuck in traffic, swap a partner, squeeze in a pair who turned up at 9:30 with cash in hand. No app has ever made that easy — most of them want everything fixed before the first serve.

But paper has a cost, and every organiser pays it on the day: the queue at the desk asking "when am I playing?", photos of the chart forwarded to six WhatsApp groups, a score that two pairs remember differently, and an evening spent adding up who paid.

Tonament was built to keep the flexibility of the sheet and take away the busywork. Here is the day, step by step.

## 1. Entries

:::compare
**On paper today**

Names come in on WhatsApp, a phone call, a form someone made. You copy them into a notebook. Fees arrive by UPI to your number, or cash at the desk, and you tick them off by memory.
---
**With Tonament**

Share one link. Players enter from their phone — singles, doubles, mixed or teams — and pay the fee **straight to your UPI**: their app opens with the amount filled in. You see a paid / unpaid list and confirm each one with a tap. Tonament never touches the money.
:::

You can still add names yourself — paste the whole WhatsApp list, one per line — and mark someone as paid in cash.

## 2. The draw

:::compare
**On paper today**

Chart paper, groups of four, levels written in the margin. Someone drops out the night before and you redraw it by hand.
---
**With Tonament**

Groups and a seeded knockout in one tap, with your list order as the seeding. **Split by level** if it's an Open event: Beginners play Beginners, and the level winners meet in the semis. Someone drops out? Redo the draw — any time before the first point.
:::

![Groups split by level: Beginner, Beginner+ and Intermediate](/guide/pools-{theme}.jpg "Each level plays its own groups; the winners meet in the semis.")

The point isn't that the app decides for you. You still decide who is Beginner+ and who is Intermediate, which semi-finals you want, and whether the top two go through or just the winner. If you get a level wrong, move the player to another group until that group has started — their matches are redone for you.

## 3. The fixtures wall

:::compare
**On paper today**

The chart goes up on the wall; a photo goes to the group. Players crowd the desk to ask when they're on, and nobody knows which court is free.
---
**With Tonament**

Every player sees the fixtures, the standings and **their own next match** on their phone. An order of play across your courts gives estimated start times, and the venue TV shows every court, who's up next and the latest results.
:::

## 4. Late pairs and changes

This is where paper usually wins — and where Tonament works hardest to keep up.

:::compare
**On paper today**

Cross out the name, draw an arrow. Shout across the hall to swap two matches. Decide on the spot whether a no-show is a walkover.
---
**With Tonament**

Tap **Not here** and their match waits while the next ready match goes on court. **Here ✓** brings them back. Never came? Give the **walkover**. A partner swap is a rename; an injury mid-match is a **retirement**; a pair who turns up late joins a group with **catch-up matches**.
:::

![The order of play with a pair marked not here yet](/guide/onday-{theme}.jpg "Ravi / Sanjay aren't here yet: their match waits, and the next one goes on.")

You stay in charge of every call. The difference is that the fixtures, the standings and the times for everyone else update themselves the moment you make it.

## 5. Scoring

:::compare
**On paper today**

Scores on scraps of paper, carried to the desk. "Was it 21-19 or 19-21?" Group tables added up late, with ties argued over.
---
**With Tonament**

Umpires score on their own phones — tap the side that won the rally — or you enter the final score at the end. The rules are built in (badminton to 21, setting to 30, who serves from where). Standings, tie-breaks and the knockout update themselves.
:::

And if the venue Wi-Fi drops, scoring keeps working: points are saved on the phone and sent when the connection is back.

## 6. Results

:::compare
**On paper today**

Winners announced, a photo of the trophy, and the sheet goes in a drawer.
---
**With Tonament**

Every result stays on the tournament page and goes on each player's profile, feeding the player rankings by city and state. Players can find your next tournament, and your page shows the history.
:::

## What stays the same

You make every call. Split levels the way you know them, swap a semi pairing, put a spot entry into the group they belong in, give a walkover or don't. Tonament simply does the arithmetic and tells everyone.
